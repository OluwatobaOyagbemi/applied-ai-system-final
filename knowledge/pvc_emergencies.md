Paraphrased summary. General guidance, not veterinary advice.
**Safety-filtered:** drug names and doses from the original pages are excluded
(see knowledge/SOURCES.md).

- If you believe it is an emergency, **contact a veterinarian immediately**.
  Ask your vet ahead of time how they handle emergencies.
- Stay calm. Muzzle or wrap an injured pet before moving it, even a gentle one,
  and move it on a board or blanket.
- **Bleeding:** apply direct pressure with a gauze pad and snug bandage; see a
  vet for deep or heavy bleeding. Don't put cotton in a wound.
- **Heat stroke** (heavy panting, collapse): cool with lukewarm (not cold)
  water and get to a vet quickly.
- **Choking:** carefully try to clear the mouth; get veterinary help if the
  object doesn't come out.
- **Poisoning:** bring the product or its label to the vet. Do **not** induce
  vomiting unless a vet or animal poison control line tells you to.
- **Seizures:** keep the pet from hurting itself, don't put fingers in its
  mouth, and call the vet.
- **Shock** (gray gums, cold, weak): keep the pet warm and quiet; go to a vet
  immediately.
- **Eye injuries:** never put medication in an injured eye without a vet.
- Diarrhea lasting more than 24 hours needs a vet and a stool check.

## Planner rules from this source
PawPal+ is a daily planner, not an emergency tool. If an owner's request
describes emergency signs, the system must stop planning and tell them to
contact a vet or emergency clinic.
