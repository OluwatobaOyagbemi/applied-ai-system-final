Paraphrased summary. General guidance, not veterinary advice.

## Physical exams
- Pets age much faster than people and often hide early signs of disease, so
  regular exams catch problems early.
- The brochure recommends a physical exam **at least every 6 months** (it also
  says adult dogs and cats need a complete exam **every year** at minimum).
- Puppies and kittens should be seen about **every 2 weeks** during their
  initial vaccination series.
- For pets over 5, annual blood work and urinalysis help detect problems early.
- Exams cover weight and body condition, skin and coat, eyes, ears, nose,
  mouth and teeth, heart and lungs, joints, and abdomen.

## Vaccinations
- Core diseases include rabies (dogs and cats), distemper and parvovirus
  (dogs), and panleukopenia and respiratory viruses (cats). Even indoor pets can
  be exposed.
- Young pets are **at risk until the full vaccine series is complete**; return
  on time for each appointment.
- The vaccine schedule must be set by the pet's own veterinarian based on age,
  breed, health, and local risk. The clinic advises against owners giving
  vaccines themselves.
- Keep a rabies tag and ID tag on the dog's collar.

## Planner rules from this source
A planner may remind the owner to book or attend vet visits and vaccine
appointments, but must not decide which vaccines a pet needs or when.
