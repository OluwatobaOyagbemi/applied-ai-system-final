Paraphrased summary. General guidance, not veterinary advice. Product names in
the original brochure are omitted on purpose; the owner's vet chooses products.

## Fleas and ticks
- Fleas are the most common parasite problem in dogs and cats. Adult fleas live
  on the pet; eggs fall into carpets and bedding, and the full life cycle
  usually takes 3-4 weeks.
- Control means treating the **pet, the house, and the yard** at the same time,
  and treating **every pet in the household** together.
- The brochure recommends a vet-recommended topical preventive applied
  **monthly**. The specific product and schedule must come from the owner's vet.
- Vacuum the house thoroughly **at least once a week** to remove eggs.

## Heartworm
- Heartworm is spread by mosquitoes, occurs throughout the US and Canada, and
  infects dogs and cats of all breeds, indoors and out.
- The brochure strongly recommends a **monthly heartworm preventive, year-round**,
  plus an **annual heartworm blood test** for dogs, because no preventive is
  100% effective.

## Intestinal worms
- Puppies and kittens are usually born with or quickly pick up worms; clinics
  deworm them several times during early visits.
- Have a fecal (internal parasite) exam done about every 6 months, and deworm at
  least once a year, as directed by the vet.

## Planner rules from this source
Any task that involves giving a medication or preventive is a reminder of a
vet's prescription only. The planner must not name products, doses, or start a
new medication, and these tasks need the owner's explicit approval.
