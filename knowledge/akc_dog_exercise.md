Paraphrased summary. General guidance, not veterinary advice.

## Puppies
Several short walks or play sessions a day are better than one long walk, which
can be too hard on a growing body. A common rule of thumb is about five minutes
of walking per month of age, once or twice a day. Running and jogging should wait
until growth plates mature: roughly 6-8 months for small breeds, 12 months for
medium, 12-18 months for large and 18-24 months for giant breeds.

## Adult dogs
Needs depend on breed and health. For a less active breed, one 30-minute walk a
day may be enough. Active breeds may need two or three longer walks, and
high-energy breeds such as Labrador Retrievers and Border Collies need at least an
hour of vigorous exercise most days. Flat-faced breeds (French Bulldogs, Pugs)
may not handle long sessions; shorter walks suit them better. A dog with hip
dysplasia or a heart or breathing condition needs a routine set with the vet.

## Senior dogs
Swap runs for walks and keep activity low-impact: gentle walks, swimming and
shorter play. Avoid jumps and sudden stops and starts. At least two outings a day
help with both mental and physical stimulation.

## General
Swimming is low-impact and good for dogs with joint problems. Don't push a dog
too hard on stairs. Mental stimulation (puzzles, training) helps but does not
replace physical exercise. Boredom or destructive behavior can be a sign of too
little activity. See the vet before starting a new exercise routine.
