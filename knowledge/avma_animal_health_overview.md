Paraphrased summary for retrieval. This is general guidance, not veterinary advice.

## Preventive care
Veterinary teams care for animals before they are sick, not only after. Regular
preventive exams are part of keeping a pet healthy. A care plan should treat
routine checkups as recurring tasks, not one-off events.

## Nutrition and weight
Owners should work with their veterinarian to make sure a pet gets nutritious
food and to manage the pet's weight. Feeding tasks should follow the diet and
portions the owner's veterinarian recommends.

## Behavior and training
Veterinarians give guidance on behavior and training. Daily enrichment and
training sessions support a pet's welfare.

## Medications and the veterinarian-client-patient relationship (VCPR)
The VCPR lets a veterinarian make recommendations and give medical care based
on each animal's needs. Medication, dosing, and treatment decisions belong to
the pet's veterinarian. A planner may remind an owner to give a medication the
vet prescribed, but it must not choose drugs, doses, or treatments itself.

## Dental health
Dental care is a recognized part of pet health (AVMA promotes National Pet
Dental Health Month each February). Regular tooth care can be planned as a
recurring grooming task.

## Microchips
AVMA promotes "Check the Chip Day": owners should confirm their pet's microchip
is registered and the contact details are current. This fits as an occasional
(for example, yearly) task.

## Disaster preparedness
Owners should prepare for emergencies before they happen (for example, keeping
supplies and records ready). This fits as an occasional planning task.

## Welfare
Animal welfare means considering every part of an animal's wellbeing, including
health, housing, and the human-animal bond.
